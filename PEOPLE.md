# People — design and plan

Click a province and see who governed it that year. Click the governor and see his
career, his family and where his evidence comes from. Then keep going.

This is the plan for bringing the Roman elite into the map, from the Digital
Prosopography of the Roman Republic (DPRR). It sits beside DESIGN.md, which stays the
source of truth for the principles (§1): nothing is drawn that the data does not
support, everything is dated, and builds report their own doubt.

Status: phase 1 (ingest) done, 23 September 2026. Phase 2 next.

---

## 1. Decisions

- **The source is DPRR**, King's College London (romanrepublic.ac.uk). It aligns
  Broughton's *Magistrates of the Roman Republic*, Rüpke's *Fasti Sacerdotum*,
  Zmeskal's *Adfinitas* and others into one linked dataset.
- **All 4,876 people ship,** from 509 BCE on, not only those alive in our window.
  The data is small, and ancestors are what make the family links work. The UI
  filters to people active in the year shown.
- **English names come from Wikidata** (CC0), through its DPRR-ID property P6863.
  Nobody searches for "Cn. Pompeius Magnus"; they search for "Pompey".
- **Person ids are namespaced:** `dprr:2072` is Cicero. People DPRR does not cover,
  such as client kings (Deiotarus, Herod), will later be our own records in `data/`
  as `ours:deiotarus`, and the app treats both the same way.

## 2. Licence: two layers, never merged

DPRR is **CC BY-NC 4.0** ("Copyright 2017 King's College London … made available
under … CC BY-NC 4.0"). Our data is **CC BY-SA 4.0** (DESIGN §2). These cannot be
combined in one dataset: share-alike would relicense DPRR's material on terms that
allow commercial use, which NC forbids.

- DPRR-derived data lives only in `build/people/`, generated, with its own `LICENSE`.
  **No DPRR fact is ever written into `data/`.**
- What we author about DPRR is ours, and lives in `data/`: the crosswalk from DPRR's
  provinces to our polities (phase 3) refers to DPRR by id and is CC BY-SA.
- Every person panel credits DPRR, links the person's DPRR page and the licence, and
  says we normalised the data. DPRR's own citations (Broughton, Rüpke, Zmeskal) are
  shown with each assertion.
- The project is non-commercial, so this is fine. If that ever changes, the people
  layer needs KCL's permission. Unlike FABDEM, it cannot simply be rebuilt without.

## 3. What DPRR holds (queried 2026-09-23)

| Table | Rows | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person | 4,876 | Praenomen, nomen, cognomen, filiation, RE number, era, sex, patrician / *nobilis* / *novus*, origin, highest office |
| PostAssertion | 9,807 | **One row per person, office and year.** Office, dates, source, Broughton's original entry |
| PostAssertionProvince | 4,097 | Links a post to one of 92 "provinces" |
| RelationshipAssertion | 6,928 | 36 types. **3,567 flagged uncertain.** No dates |
| StatusAssertion | 1,992 | Senator or eques, dated |
| Office | 204 | Hierarchical (`hasParent`) |
| Province | 92 | Hierarchical (`hasParent`) |
| SecondarySource | 32 | Broughton MRR I–III, Rüpke, Zmeskal, Badian 1990 … |

Uncertainty is recorded throughout: `isUncertain`, `isDateStartUncertain`,
`isDateEndUncertain`, `isProvinceUncertain`, and flags on each name part. All of it
is kept.

Access: a public SPARQL endpoint (`https://romanrepublic.ac.uk/rdf/endpoint`) that
answers scripted queries, and a full RDF export. The website itself sits behind a bot
check; the endpoint does not.

### 3.1 Traps

- **DPRR's years are not astronomical.** Cicero's consulship is `-63`, meaning
  63 BCE; his birth is `-106`. Ours is −62 and −105. The import converts every
  negative year (`y → y + 1`), and a test pins Cicero's consulship to 63 BCE. This is
  the trap of DESIGN §5.2, arriving from outside.
- **Relationships are stored in both directions,** often one of them marked
  "Autogenerated by add_inverse". Cicero *son of* Helvia and Helvia *mother of*
  Cicero are two rows. The build collapses each pair into one edge using DPRR's own
  inverse table, which is keyed by relationship **and sex** (*father of* inverts to
  *son of* or *daughter of*).
- **A province row includes the governor's staff.** Cilicia in 51 BCE returns Cicero
  as proconsul, plus seven legates and a quaestor. Offices are classified by role
  (§4.2) so the governor can be told apart from his staff.
- **"Province" is not always ground.** The 92 include:
  - territories (Asia, Cilicia);
  - Italian regions and cities (Samnium, Capua);
  - courts (repetundae, de vi);
  - praetorian jurisdictions (urbanus, inter peregrinos);
  - commands (Mediterranean, fleet, "cum imperio consulari infinito");
  - vague areas (Hispania, Gallia, Italia).
  
  The crosswalk (phase 3) gives each a kind.
- **Gaps are real.** Cilicia has no governor attested for 65–57, while Pompey's
  eastern command was in force. "No governor attested" is an answer, not a blank.
- **Defeated candidacies are posts.** `repulsa (cos.)` and the like record an
  election someone *lost*. They are career events, never office held.
- **A few years lost their minus sign.** Three office ranges read like `-99 … 95`
  (99–95 BCE). Four statuses run into CE: three rightly (Augustus to 14 CE, Asinius
  Pollio to 4 CE, Sempronius Atratinus to 7 CE), and one wrongly (Maecenas, who died
  in 8 BCE). A rule cannot tell these apart, so each is a named correction (§4.1).
- **DPRR's "era" for each person is unreliable.** 507 people have an era ending in
  0, which is not a year in DPRR's numbering, and 787 more end on a positive year that
  is sometimes CE and sometimes a lost minus sign. We do not use it where either end
  is ambiguous (§4.1).
- **DPRR's table of opposite relationships has holes.** It says what B is when A is
  *father of* B, depending on B's sex. For a female grandchild or great-grandchild
  four of its rows give "grandson" or "great grandson", and nine types (stepbrother,
  halfbrother, great uncle …) have no rows at all. Corrected and filled in (§4.1).
- **DPRR contradicts itself once:** C. Octavius Rufus and C. Octavius are each
  recorded as the other's son. Both are kept, and the build reports it.

## 4. Build outputs

```
pipeline/src/people/
  dprr.ts        fetch: SPARQL → .cache/sources/dprr/*.json + manifest
  wikidata.ts    fetch: P6863 → .cache/sources/dprr/wikidata.json
  offices.ts     our classification of DPRR's offices by role
  corrections.ts our corrections to DPRR, each with its reason
  build.ts       cache → build/people/
packages/schema/src/people.ts   the baked types, shared by pipeline and app
build/people/                   DPRR-derived, CC BY-NC 4.0
  persons.json  posts.json  relationships.json  statuses.json  lifeEvents.json
  authorities.json  search.json  manifest.json  LICENSE
```

- `npm run people:fetch -w pipeline` queries DPRR and Wikidata once and caches the
  answers. It is never run by an ordinary build: KCL's server is not our CDN.
  Wikimedia asks scripts to give a contact, so the fetch sends `ROMAN_MAP_CONTACT`
  from the gitignored `.env.local` at the repo root. It is never in the code.
- `npm run people -w pipeline` builds from the cache in under a second. It prints the
  counts, every correction it applied, and anything it could not classify, merge or
  resolve.
- The app loads `build/people/` lazily after the first frame (DESIGN §10 budget).
  It is about 6 MB raw; `persons.json`, the largest, is about 320 KB gzipped.

### 4.1 Normalisation

- Years become astronomical, in one function (`dprrYear`) with a test that pins
  Cicero's consulship to 63 BCE. DPRR's uncertainty flags are carried as `uncertain`
  on each endpoint.
- **A person's `active` span** comes from their dated posts, statuses and life events
  (births, deaths, exiles, proscriptions). Only when there are none does it fall back
  to DPRR's era, and then only if both ends are BCE. 3,741 people are dated by their
  records, 910 by their era, and 225 not at all: those are reached through their
  relatives, never by the year filter.
- **Corrections** (`corrections.ts`) each name the value they expect to find. If a
  later fetch shows DPRR has changed that value, the build reports the correction as
  out of date instead of overwriting whatever is there.
- Names:
  - `name`, the short display form: "M. Tullius Cicero";
  - `full`, the full nomenclature: "M. Tullius M. f. M. n. Cor. Cicero";
  - `re`, the RE number that tells namesakes apart: Cicero is RE 29, his son RE 30.
  - Wikidata adds `en` (label, aliases, description) where it has the person.
- Relationships become one edge per pair: 6,928 DPRR rows become 3,465 edges. Each
  edge keeps every source, the ancient citations, the uncertain flag and DPRR's notes,
  and lists the DPRR rows merged into it. Two relationships have no reverse row and
  stay single.
- Every assertion keeps its secondary source, Broughton's original entry text and,
  where DPRR has them, its ancient citations ("Diod. XXXVIII 12.").
- Wikidata supplies English names for 2,962 of the 4,876 people (61%): "Pompey",
  "Mark Antony", "Augustus" (with "Octavian" among its aliases). Some popular short
  names are only aliases ("Crassus", "Clodius", "Brutus"), so search must match
  aliases too. Minor figures often share a full Latin label (twelve "Marcus Claudius
  Marcellus"), so results must show dates and offices beside the name.

### 4.2 Office roles (ours, `offices.ts`)

| Role | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `command` | Held imperium, so could govern a province | consul, praetor, proconsul, propraetor, promagistrate, dictator, legatus pro praetore |
| `staff` | Served under someone who held command | legatus (lieutenant), quaestor, proquaestor, praefectus, tribunus militum |
| `magistracy` | A civil office without a provincial command, or elected but not yet in office | tribunus plebis, aedilis, censor, monetalis, consul designatus |
| `commission` | Envoys and the Senate's commissions | legatus (ambassador), land commissioners, iudex |
| `priesthood` | | pontifex, augur, flamen, virgo Vestalis |
| `candidacy` | An election lost | repulsa (cos.) |
| `distinction` | An honour or standing, not an office | triumphator, princeps senatus, senator (office unknown) |
| `equestrian` | Business roles of the equestrian order | publicanus, magister societatis |
| `other` | Anything unclassified: the build lists it | |

Rules follow DPRR's own tree of offices: a rule names an office or a whole branch, and
the nearest one up the tree wins.

Every DPRR office must be classified. A new one in a later fetch shows up in the
build's report as `other`.

## 5. Phases

1. **Ingest** (done):
   - fetch DPRR and Wikidata;
   - normalise;
   - bake `build/people/`;
   - tests for the year conversion, the office roles, relationship merging, names,
     corrections, and checks against the real build (Cicero consul in 63 BCE; in
     Cilicia in 51 BCE, Cicero commands and his brother Quintus is staff).
2. **Person panel and ⌘K search**, with no map join:
   - career as a dated list;
   - family grouped (parents, spouses, children, adoptions), uncertain links marked;
   - sources and the DPRR credit;
   - `?person=dprr:2072` in the URL.
3. **Crosswalk and governors:**
   - `data/dprr-provinces.yaml`, one entry per DPRR province, with its kind and,
     for territories, a dated list of our polities;
   - the territory inspector shows the governor for the year, with staff folded
     away and his Broughton reference, or "No governor attested";
   - the build reports each province's unattested years.
4. **Career on the map:** highlight the provinces of a career and jump to their
   years. This needs one new engine call, `map.highlight(polityIds, style)`.
5. **Later:**
   - portraits via Wikidata;
   - a family graph;
   - Cicero's correspondents;
   - our own `person` records for client kings.

## 6. Open questions

- **Two governors in one year is normal, not an error.** Cilicia in 51 BCE has
  Appius Claudius Pulcher (outgoing) and Cicero (incoming) both in command. DPRR
  works in whole years, so phase 3 must show both, in order where the sources allow.

- DPRR has 16k notes on posts and people; phase 1 leaves them out. Show them in the
  person panel later, or link to DPRR's page?
- How to show a relationship DPRR marks uncertain: a "?" beside it, or a faded row
  as on the map?
