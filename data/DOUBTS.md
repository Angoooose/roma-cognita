# Historical doubts

What the map's historical data is unsure of: where the sources disagree, where a
line or a date is a convention, and what is left undrawn for want of evidence.
Each record affected carries its own `review` status (`draft` or `needs-check`),
its confidence, and a `note` with the reasoning; this file gathers the open
questions by region. `npm run data:validate` prints every record still to check.

## Italy and the islands

- _How Italy is split before the Social War_ (README decision 22). The frontier
  between the citizen land and the allies is a zone, drawn as a line after
  Beloch's inferences from colonies, prefectures and the later voting tribes
  (1880, ch. III). Beloch puts Tarquinii, Vulci and Falerii in the Roman land to
  the Umbro and Monte Amiata (others keep Tarquinii and Vulci allied), and the
  strip along the Via Flaminia past Asisium. The Frentani and Caraceni, the
  Samnite, Hirpine and Apulian lines, and the Laus–Crathis frontier are
  conventions; all eleven of these atoms are schematic and uncertain.
- _The allies are one polity_, `socii`, not a dozen peoples: Rome's treaties
  were with some 150 communities (Beloch ch. VIII), and no source draws the
  peoples' frontiers.
- _Enclaves not drawn_: the Latin colonies (a place status), Tibur and
  Praeneste, Neapolis and Capri, Ancona, Teanum Sidicinum, Camerinum, Ravenna;
  Nola, which held out until 80 (Per. 89); Volaterrae (to c. 80 or 79).
- _The dates of the Social War_: Etruria, the heel and the toe enfranchised in
  90; the Marsic peoples, Asculum, the Nucerine cities and the Hirpini in 89
  (the Marsi may have held into 88); Apulia in 88; Samnium and Lucania in 87
  (App. BC 1.68, Per. 80), though Gabinius took many Lucanian towns in 89.
  Beloch has most of Tiber-valley Umbria (Tuder, Iguvium, Arna, Ameria) join
  the rising in 90–89 (Per. 74); it is drawn loyal, as no source dates its
  submission. Calabria is drawn loyal because no source puts it in the revolt;
  Bruttium is drawn allied though much of it was Roman public land. The
  Samnites at the Colline Gate in 82 are not drawn.
- _Cisalpina_: Cispadana is drawn as citizen land before 91, a mixture drawn as
  its larger part, which sweeps in allied Faesulae, the Veleiates and Sarsina;
  Beloch (p. 67) has most of the Ligurian plain west of the Trebia already
  Roman, though it is drawn allied. Transpadana has Latin rights from 89
  (Asconius, Pis. 3 C) and is allied to 82. The province is drawn from 81–80,
  Sulla's reforms; others put it in the 90s or in 89.
- _Cisalpina's end_ is drawn at the legal decision after Philippi (42; App. BC
  5.3). In practice Pollio held Venetia for Antony into 40 (Vell. 2.76.2);
  Laffi (1992, not read) reportedly argues for late 41.
- _The Sicilian slave kingdoms_ are seats (Enna, Triocala), since nothing
  records how far their power reached inside a province Rome still governed;
  the Triocala site is Pleiades' doubtful identification. Spartacus (73–71) is
  not drawn.
- _Sextus Pompeius._ Dio 48.2.1 has him "still occupying Sardinia and Sicily"
  in 42, against the seizure of Sardinia in 40 in his own narrative and
  Appian's; it is read as an anticipation. When he took Corsica is not
  recorded; it is drawn his by 40, with Sardinia.
- _Places_: Mariana's founding ({103–86}) and Pompeii's colony (c. 80) are not
  dated by any source; Tarentum's municipium from 90 rests on App. BC 1.49, and
  when Neptunia was joined to the city is Beloch's "Sulla or Augustus";
  Hydruntum's status rests on a general page of Beloch (p. 175). Víteliú on the
  insurgents' coins is not cited from a coin catalogue.
- _Not drawn_: the civil war of 88–82 and any Sullan command in Italy; the
  Cimbri in Transpadana (102–101); which triumvir held which province.

## Greece and the Balkans

- _Achaea in 27_ is its first firm attestation, not its creation (Sulpicius
  Rufus governed it in 46–45). Thessaly in it rests on Bowersock's emended text
  of Strabo 17.3.25, and Strabo's list is Tiberian, not the list of 27; how far
  north its Epirus reached is a convention (the Aous).
- _Mithridates in Greece_: Achaea is occupied 88–86, to the Delium terms of
  winter 86/85, when Archelaus withdrew his garrisons (Mithr. 55); Macedonia
  and the Chersonese only in 87 (Arcathias' army came down to Thermopylae in
  86). Sentius and the Denseletae held out. Sparta keeps its free status
  through 88–86 though it sided with Mithridates (Mithr. 29); Athens under
  Aristion has none.
- _The Caenic land._ Pliny puts Caenica inland near Bizye and Apros (4.47),
  beyond the drawn Chersonese; the Knidos copy's "which Didius took" may govern
  only the Caenic land. The Chersonese was the Attalids' in 133 and Rome's hold
  on it before c. 100 is unrecorded, so it is unclaimed. Sumner dates the law
  late 100 or early 99.
- _The Iapydes' atom_: the Colapis and the Una are conventions, and Strabo
  says the Colapis flowed through their country (7.5.2).
- _The Delmatae as a province from 33_: a tributary people under treaty is as
  defensible; they revolted in 16 and 11.
- _The Sapaean kingdom's Aegean coast_ between the Nestus and the Hebrus lies
  inside the macedonia atom.
- _Thrace before 90 and Noricum before 113_ are unclaimed: those years are
  first attestations, not beginnings.
- _Not drawn_: the Illyrian coast before 59 ("Roman Illyria", App. Ill. 10–11,
  with no governor recorded); Tuditanus' Iapydes (129) and Metellus' Segestani
  (119); the Scordisci's raids to Delphi; Curio's and M. Lucullus' wars; Getic
  Dacia before Burebista; the garrison at Siscia (35), Pannonia, Moesia
  (tributary only under Tiberius, App. Ill. 30), the Dacian and Getic kingdoms
  after Burebista, and Brutus' takeover of Sadalas' bequest in 43/42 (Dio
  47.25.1). Two allies of Rome with no ground drawn are not polities: Sitas of
  the Dentheletae, "under treaty" (Dio 51.23.4; long-standing allies by Cic.
  Pis. 84), and the Getic king Roles, "friend and ally" c. 29 (Dio 51.24.7).
- _Not yet places_: Nicopolis (31/29), Siscia, Metulum, Promona, and most of
  the western Pontic cities (the `apollonia` id is the Illyrian one).

## The Black Sea

- _The Bosporus_: c. 110–107 for the decree for Diophantus is the usual
  reckoning, the stone being dated only by a local eponym. When its peoples
  revolted is not recorded, so the Pontic row ends in 87 or 86. The Maeotic
  shore under the Spartocids and under Machares is unclaimed, though
  Plutarch's "ancient realm as far as the deserts beyond Lake Maeotis" cuts
  against that.
- _The Bosporus as Rome's client from c. 41_ follows Vnukov and Kovalenko's
  chronology of Asander's coins; ps.-Lucian (Macr. 17) says Augustus made him
  king.
- _The Crimean Scythians_ are Pontus' clients from c. 110 to 64, on the decree
  and Strabo 7.4.3, and Appian's "princes of the Scythian tribes" among the
  king's allies at the end (Mithr. 119); while the king was a refugee, in
  70–66, this is doubtful.
- _Chersonesus_: its standing under Mithridates, the Bosporan kings and Rome
  changes with every change of master (Strabo 7.4.3), and every row is
  uncertain; its freedom from Caesar's grant of 45 lasts an unknown time,
  drawn to somewhere in 45–38 (Pliny 4.85).
- _The western Pontic cities_: the Pontic protectorate of c. 110–72 rests on
  the scholarship more than on Mithr. 15's vague "Greeks bordering on the
  Euxine"; they are drawn free again from 43, when Burebista's kingdom broke
  up (Strabo 7.3.11), and nothing read states their standing after him.

## The West

- _The Province's date._ Transalpina is drawn from 121–120; the regular
  province is now often put down to Marius in the Cimbric wars or to Pompey in
  the 70s (Amela Valverde 2019, 39), and Ebel (1976, not read) has the ground
  west of the Rhône governed from Hither Spain until then. The Salluvian
  country, beaten in 124–122 and garrisoned at Aquae Sextiae, is not drawn
  apart. Before 121 the land is unclaimed but for Massalia: the Arvernian
  hegemony (Strabo 4.2.3) has no extent anyone can draw.
- _The Tolosates' alliance_ is undated and drawn from 121; their revolt of 107
  sits inside it; Caepio's sack is drawn in his consulship, 106 (Orosius'
  "proconsul" would make it 105).
- _The Ruteni provinciales_ are inside the Province from 121, though BG 1.45
  says they were not made provincial then; the 70s are argued (Amela 24, 28).
- _The Aedui before 122_ are independent, uncertain: their alliance is first
  attested in 122 (Per. 61), though Strabo 4.3.2 suggests it is older.
- _The name Gallia Narbonensis_: when it became official is unknown; it is a
  non-preferred name only.
- _Lusitania, Baetica and the three Gauls_: dated to 27 by Dio, to 16–13 by
  most modern work; not drawn (README decision 21).
- _Sertorius_ (a `command`, README decision 18) is drawn over Hither Spain and
  the Arevaci from c. 77 (Plut. Sert. 16.1) to 73, uncertain: the coast towns
  stayed with the government (Pompey's letter, Sall. Hist. 2.98 M), and Strabo
  has him driven from Celtiberia before his death. His years in Lusitania and
  Further Spain (80–78) draw no ground; his death is 73 or 72.
- _Lusitania between the Tagus and the Durius_ is unclaimed until 61–60,
  despite Brutus' surrender of 138–136 and its ten commissioners (App. Hisp.
  99).
- _The Spains in 41–40_: Octavian's on Dio 48.1.3, Antony's on App. BC 5.51.
- _The Vaccaei c. 29_ rests on Dio's undated account of Statilius Taurus; the
  Turmogi and Autrigones, probably Rome's by 26, stay unclaimed.
- _The Salassi_: "subjugated" in 34 (App. Ill. 17) against Strabo 4.6.7 and
  Dio's "revolt" of 25; left unclaimed, with Cottius' realm, whose standing
  before 14 is unknown.
- _The Gallic oppida_ start before 133 only on Pleiades' period attestation;
  Bibracte (before 100) and Gergovia (69–60) show how wrong that can be.
- _Not drawn_: the Cimbri and Teutones (109–102), who crossed the land without
  holding it; Pompey's grants of Volcan and Helvian land to Massalia (BC 1.35);
  Pompey's trophy at the Col de Panissars (no place kind fits a monument).
- _Not yet places_: most of the Gallic colonies of 43–27.

## Africa

- _The division of 117–116_: Adherbal's western limit is unknown (Bridoux
  ¶141), so the three atoms between the Ampsaga and the Chelif are unclaimed
  in 116–113.
- _The Jugurthine War_ is the kingdom's independence, 111–106; the Roman
  garrisons (Vaga, Thala, Cirta) and Capsa's destruction are not drawn.
- _Bocchus I's gain of 105_ is taken to Cap Ténès (Gsell; Bridoux ¶75, ¶145);
  Desanges carried it at least to the mouth of the Chelif; Sallust's "a third
  of Numidia" is a promise.
- _Mauretania to the Soummam_ from c. 80 rests on Desanges and Bridoux ¶97,
  ¶102, and could be as late as the 50s; unclaimed c. 88–81. Masteabar's
  standing to Rome (c. 88–82) is taken to be his father's.
- _Arabio's lands to Bocchus in 41_ is Bridoux's inference (¶103).
- _Mauretania 33–25_: a province on Dio's words and to Mackie, Amandry and
  Amela Valverde; a limbo or protectorate to Gsell, Roller,
  Coltelloni-Trannoy and Hugoniot. Its colonies are not yet places.
- _The two Africas' union_ is drawn at 27; Fishwick and Shaw (1977) and
  Fishwick (1993, 1994) argue for 36/35.
- _Cyrenaica_: the Ptolemies' to 108–106, Apion's from 105–100 (Justin puts his
  accession at 116). The free cities of 96–75 are drawn `allied` for want of a
  closer standing; Sallust's quaestor "to the new province" (Hist. 2.43 M)
  falls in 75, against the province's c. 74. Cleopatra Selene's Cyrenaica,
  34–31, stands against a Roman quaestor's coins dated only "34–31 (?)" (RPC I
  919–923).
- _Not drawn_: Hiarbas (82/81), the Marian hold on Africa in 82–81, Sertorius
  at Tingi in 81, Iunonia (122–121), Marius' Gaetulian settlements, a Juban
  Numidia in 30–25 (Dio 51.15.6 against 53.26.2), and the "parts of Crete"
  given to Cleopatra's children (Dio 49.32.5).

## Egypt, Cyprus and Nubia

- _Egypt a client from 59_, at the recognition, not the garrison of 55; its
  civil wars and the Theban revolt of c. 88–86 are not drawn.
- _Cyprus_: Ptolemy X's kingdom from 114 and Ptolemy IX's from 105 rest on
  Bennett's reading of Mitford and Mørkholm; Bennett doubts the usual view that
  Ptolemy X went first as its general; Ptolemy of Cyprus from 80 is inferred
  from Trogus' Prologue 40. In 41–37 it was Cleopatra's (Schrapel), Antony's
  (Bicknell), or both (Cayla); it is drawn hers.
- _Lower Nubia_: Eller (2025) has Ptolemy VI re-establish control to the Second
  Cataract and the first century "difficult to determine", and questions the
  Kushite row even for 68–30. A Ptolemaic Dodecaschoenus before 30, which
  Burstein's boundary at Hierasykaminos (Barrington map 81) would support, is
  not drawn.
- _The Triacontaschoenus_ rests on one inscription, the Philae stele; its Latin
  and Greek differ on Kush (under protection, or a friend). The rulers of Meroë
  in 43–29 are not named: Fontes Historiae Nubiorum was not consulted.
- _Unread_: the law of 101/100 (Crawford, Roman Statutes 12), SEG IX 5, OGIS
  166, known only through Bennett; Braund 1984 and Török 1997.

## Anatolia

- _Asia from 133._ Strabo has the province proclaimed at the bequest (13.4.2);
  129 is the usual date for its organisation, and at first the tribute-paying
  ground was "a patchwork … chiefly in Mysia and Lydia" (Kallet-Marx 112–115).
  The whole atom is drawn from 133 as a convention.
- _Aristonicus_ is a seat at Stratonicea on the Caicus, not an occupation: he
  held towns and forts, not a territory. Coarelli argues for the Carian
  Stratonicea.
- _Greater Phrygia_: Aquillius' grant to Pontus may fall anywhere in 129–126,
  and its return to Asia is drawn {119–116}, uncertain; Kallet-Marx (240–242)
  has it freed, not annexed, and taken in only after c. 95.
- _Lycaonia's_ return to Asia is placed at the law of 101/100 and its passage
  to Cilicia at Servilius' Isaurian war (75), an inference.
- _Cilicia before 80_: whether a territory or only a command against the
  pirates is argued (Sherwin-White, n. 18, for a base in Pamphylia; Borgia
  299–300 for a sphere of competence); drawn uncertain from {102–100}.
  Zenicetus' Lycian strongholds are not drawn.
- _The Cilician plain_ is Armenian from 83 on Appian's fourteen years; Justin
  has eighteen and some modern work later dates. It is unclaimed in 69–68.
- _Cilicia's end in 44/43_ follows Syme and Magie; Bickerman (1947) held the
  plain joined Syria only after AD 18. The plain is Syria's from 43, uncertain
  to 38.
- _Pontus_: c. 120 and c. 113 are modern reckonings. Paphlagonia's partition
  of c. 108 is told in a note, not drawn: which king held what is unknown, and
  the senate took Paphlagonia from Nicomedes (Justin 38.2). Pontus is drawn
  `allied` to 89 on Appian's "friend and ally". Lucullus' hold of 71–69 is a
  Roman occupation, not a province: the commissioners came only in 67.
  Heraclea's Pontic garrison of 73–71/70 and Mytilene's holding out to 81/80
  are not drawn.
- _Cappadocia_: Tigranes' first expulsion of Ariobarzanes (Sherwin-White's, c.
  95–93) is not drawn; Justin's "first approach of Tigranes" is read as the
  expulsion of 90 (Kallet-Marx 356–359). The 81–79 occupation covers only "a
  greater part" of the kingdom but is drawn whole; Tigranes' raid of 78 or 77
  and the overrunning of 67–66 are not drawn. Ariobarzanes III died in 43
  (Appian) or 42 (Dio); whether Sisines is Archelaus, and whether he was
  installed in 41 or 36, is open.
- _Galatia_ is drawn as Rome's ally before 64, uncertain (its standing is
  nowhere stated), and occupied by Mithridates only in 86, when Eumachus was
  sent after the massacre (Mithr. 46).
- _Cibyra's tyranny_ ends c. 83 (Kallet-Marx 114) or c. 82 (Sherwin-White n.
  6).
- _Pharnaces' occupation of 48–47_ covers Pontus, Lesser Armenia, Colchis and
  Deiotarus' Pontic lands; the part of Cappadocia he overran is not drawn. It
  keeps 47 though Zela was fought in August 47, where every other occupation
  ends the year before the occupier was driven out (README decision 17).
- _Labienus' occupation of Asia in 40_ is drawn over the whole atom, though the
  islands and Stratonicea held out and he styled himself a Roman imperator.
- _Pontus under Antony_: what Darius held inland in 39–37 is unknown; the Zela
  temple and Ateporix's principality lie inside pontus-inner; Amisus
  (Polemon's?, then a tyrant's, free from 31) and the Amnias basin with
  Pompeiopolis (Paphlagonia's by 37/36 at the latest) stay with the coast atom.
- _Lesser Armenia 39–34_: no holder is recorded, and it is unclaimed.
- _Amyntas' kingdom_: Isauria is drawn with Lycaonia from 36 but may be
  post-Actium (Magie I 443); Apollonia, his at his death (Strabo 12.6.4), is
  inside asia-phrygia; Pamphylia and southern Pisidia are unclaimed after 43
  (his "portions of Pamphylia" may be Side, or his Pisidian lands); Rough
  Cilicia east of Seleucia, in Strabo's grant, stays with Olba.
- _Olba_ is independent from 67 and Antony's client from 39, when he and
  Cleopatra gave it to Aba (Strabo 14.5.10); whether she was his client or
  Cleopatra's dependant is not stated.
- _Derbe's end_: c. 36 (Robert), the late 30s or early 20s (Mitchell), or
  after Actium (Magie, drawn).
- _Comana_: Dio's Medeius and Strabo's Cleon may be one man.
- _Unread_: Robinson (NC 1954) on Aristonicus' cistophori; the Cnidus text
  itself; Sallust's fragment on Marcius Rex in Lycaonia; Posidonius'
  "strategos of Pamphylia"; McGing 1986.

## The Levant

- _Tigranes' Syria_ is 83–70 on Appian's fourteen years (Syr. 48); Justin has
  eighteen (40.1), and some modern work c. 74. Selene held Ptolemais to c.
  70/69 until Tigranes took it (AJ 13.419–421) and Aretas held Damascus c.
  85–72 (no passage opened), but the Syria and Phoenicia atoms are Armenian
  throughout. Tyre and Sidon stay free through his years: no source says he
  took them.
- _The last Seleucids_: the claim on all of Syria in 69–65 is doubtful, as
  Antiochus XIII hardly ruled beyond Antioch. The division from 113 (Grypus
  against Cyzicenus, and their sons), and the Nabataean and Armenian Damascus,
  are not drawn: no line for them. Antiochus VII's Babylonia (130–129) and
  Tigranes' northern Mesopotamia (Nisibis, to 68) have no ground in the
  parthia atom; nor have Characene and Adiabene.
- _The Hasmonean conquests_ are dated per atom: Idumaea from 129 (Josephus
  puts it straight after 129, the archaeology of Marisa and Gerizim c.
  112–111, not opened here); Samaria and Scythopolis from 111, though Pella,
  Dion, Gerasa and the Gaulanitis fell in the 80s; Galilee 105, on the usual
  reading of AJ 13.318 (some put it under Hyrcanus I); Peraea 104, before
  Jannaeus took Theodorus' Amathus (AJ 13.356); Strato's Tower, Dora and the
  coast 103 (Zoilus' tyranny is not drawn); Gaza 96. By 103 Iamnia and Azotus
  were already Jewish (AJ 13.324), so the southern coast may be drawn too
  late; Ascalon, never Hasmonean, lies inside that Judaean atom, its freedom
  only a place status; the gaza atom's eastern corner is Marisa's lowland,
  drawn Seleucid to 97 against AJ 13.257. Gaza is drawn standing though
  Jannaeus destroyed it (AJ 13.364) and Gabinius rebuilt it (14.87–88).
- _Moab_ is Judaean from 96–90 to c. 88, Nabataean c. 87–85 (given back in the
  civil war, AJ 13.382) and Judaean again from 84.
- _Nabataea_ is independent until Scaurus (62; AJ 14.80–81); the king who
  killed Antiochus XII is unnamed in the Greek (AJ 13.391).
- _The city eras_ of Tyre (126/125), Sidon (111) and Ascalon (104/103) rest on a
  dealer's catalogue entry, Livius.org and an abstract respectively; BMC
  Phoenicia was not opened.
- _Emesa_ is drawn from 69, the earliest the texts allow: Diodorus 40.1b, the
  first mention of Sampsigeramus, is undated (c. 65–64). In 30–27 it is drawn
  as Syria's, uncertain; no source says who governed it before Iamblichus II's
  restoration in 20. _The Amanus_ from 64 is a convention, and it is unclaimed
  in 30–20 (Dmitriev suggests Amyntas, then Archelaus).
- _Jerusalem's fall in 37_: Josephus' consular date, against his own
  "twenty-seven years" from 63 and Dio's 38.
- _Cleopatra's grants_ are drawn from 36, uncertain (Plutarch; her new count
  of years in 37/36); Josephus tells them with the events of 34, and the double
  era is Porphyry's for Chalcis only. The coast is drawn as her holding, the
  "parts of Arabia" as revenue and not at all.
- _The coast in 30–27_ outside Herod's cities is taken to have gone back to
  Syria; Ascalon, free since 104, may never have been Cleopatra's.

## Armenia, Parthia and the Caucasus

- _Armenia before 95_ is independent, uncertain: Mithridates II fought
  Artavasdes (Justin 42.2) and held Tigranes hostage (38.3), but a Parthian
  overlordship is recorded nowhere. Gordyene, a kingdom of its own until
  Tigranes took it (Strabo 11.14.15), lies inside the armenia atom and is drawn
  Armenian throughout.
- _Tigranes' conquests_ of Sophene (c. 94) and Atropatene (86) have no ancient
  year; Parthian overlordship of Atropatene before then is inferred.
- _Sophene from 66_ is drawn Cappadocia's, uncertain: Tigranes' (Pompey), his
  son's (Dio 36.53) or Cappadocia's (App. Mithr. 105).
- _Armenia a client of Rome from 66_, even under Artavasdes II, is a
  convention.
- _Armenia under Antony_: his garrison is drawn from 34 to 33–31 and Artaxias
  II from 32–30; when Artaxias came back is not recorded. The part of Armenia
  given to the Mede in 33 is not drawn.
- _Iberia and Albania_ are drawn Rome's allies from 65; nothing attests the tie
  after 36.
- _Osroene_: its start rests on the Chronicle of Edessa's first entry
  (Seleucid year 180) against Dionysius' 136, and is drawn at 134. Its tie to
  Rome ends when Abgar turned on Crassus in 53; its standing afterwards is not
  recorded, so it is left a seat with no relation.
- _Commagene_'s Antiochus I may have died c. 37–36 or as late as 31 (Dio
  49.23.4 is disputed).
- _Phraates IV_ "came into power shortly before 37" (Debevoise 121); whether 38
  or 37 is open.
- _Not drawn_: Colchis under Polemon (undated) and Tiridates II.

## Territory outlines

Every outline follows the frontiers its cited works name (atoms.yaml;
`npm run atoms:preview -w pipeline` draws the review plates). These choices
want a reader's eye:

- **Conventions, not frontiers.** Numidia's and Mauretania's southern edges,
  Cyrenaica's, Egypt's and Nabataea's desert edges, Galatia's and Cappadocia's
  inner lines, Cilicia's northern edge, the Thracian kingdom and Illyricum's
  inland edge are drawn where authority faded; Bridoux says of the Numidian
  south that it was "probablement pas précisément établie". Nabataea, Odrysian
  Thrace and Ituraea are the weakest.
- **Neighbours whose extent is a convention:** Dacia (Burebista's widest
  reach; Lockyear doubts any fixed extent), Noricum, the Garamantes, Kush, the
  Caucasian kingdoms, Atropatene, Parthia beyond the frame, the Gallic peoples
  (after the later civitas lines, Fichtl), and the four Iberian peoples (after
  their Augustan conventus; Strabo's Melsus is taken as the Sella).
- **One scholar's hypothesis.** Mauretania east of Cap Ténès to the Soummam
  (Desanges, accepted by Bridoux ¶102) is `uncertain`; Gsell's line at Cap
  Ténès is the cautious alternative.
- **The fossa regia south of Abthugni** follows Tissot's line along the
  sebkhas; Saumagne's is more westerly (Bridoux ¶133–134). The Tusca is the
  oued Zouara (Bridoux ¶127), not the oued el-Kebir Pleiades puts it at.
- **Citerior/Ulterior north of the Tagus** has an attested direction and no
  course (Richardson).
- **The Rubicon** is the modern Rubicone, whose identification is
  conventional.
- **Castabala** is both Tarcondimotus' seat and, in Strabo, a gift to
  Ariobarzanes; it is drawn with the Amanus.
- **Left unclaimed on purpose:** the Cottian and Maritime Alps and Raetia;
  Istria; the Cyclades, Lemnos and Imbros; the Tracheia west of Olba before
  37/36; Siwa and inner Sinai; the Propontic Greek cities; Gaul beyond the four
  peoples drawn, before 51; the Autrigones, Vascones and their neighbours
  between the Cantabri and Pompaelo; the steppe north of the Crimea.
- **Inside a larger atom:** Issa (Illyricum); the Suebi's third of the Sequani
  (BG 1.31).

## Floodplains and the Nile branches

- **Published areas are unchecked.** Butzer 1976 (Egypt), Adams 1981
  (Mesopotamia) and the Ptolemaic Fayum figures are cited as "usually cited";
  the books have not been opened. Egypt comes out at about 35,000 km² against
  the 22,000–34,000 range, mostly because the delta includes the northern
  lagoon margins that were marsh in antiquity.
- **Fayum lake level** (−2 m) and the canal-command bound (+26 m) are single
  numbers for a lake that moved during the window.
- **Gulf coast**: Mesopotamia and Susiana stop at the head of the Gulf as
  persian-gulf-head draws it, a middle line in a debate that runs from the
  modern coast to Charax; ±15–25 km.
- **Branch courses** between sites are splines; the Sebennytic lower course,
  the Mendesian and the Tanitic are `uncertain`. Where the Canopic leaves the
  Rosetta is our choice.
- **Hull edges that are boundaries, not terrain**: valley/delta at Memphis,
  Fayum/valley at Hawara, Mesopotamia/Susiana, Susiana's south-east.

## Sea names

- **Pliny is cited to the book only**, and he wrote a century after the
  window. The Republic's own usage was looser: Caesar has only "the Ocean" for
  the North Sea. The Barrington Atlas's forms are used throughout.
- **The courses are placement, not extent.** Where one sea ends and the next
  begins (Ionium against Siculum, Libycum against Aegyptium) is not claimed.

## The city of Rome

- **Only six valley floors are set from excavated levels**
  (pipeline/src/rome/ground.ts). The Subura, the Circus' valley, the Velabrum
  and the valleys of the north keep today's terrain, though they too filled
  up: a published map of the depth of the fill in Rome's centre, or more dated
  levels, would let them be set.
- **Two of the six are ceilings.** The Forum's square and the valley of the
  Colosseum are set from levels of the early empire; the Republican ground lay
  at or a little below them.
- **The Velia stood higher** than today (cut in 1932) but is not raised:
  SITAR's levels there are measured against a terrain model too uncertain to
  trust.

## Beyond 133 and 27 BCE

The window's edges are written `before 133 BCE` / `after 27 BCE`. Extending it
means replacing each open end with a real date, which reopens every record
that carries one. One open end inside the window is left on purpose: the
Kushite hold on Lower Nubia, `before 68 BCE`, which nothing dates earlier.
